Two things the next leg must not relearn the hard way:
  * While SimHost.TimeScale is 0 the PRESENTER does not move anyone. Writing a man's position or yaw into the world
    while frozen changes nothing on screen (SimProbe then shows "sim at" and "drawn at" disagreeing). Every staging
    write has to happen with the sim running, and the freeze comes last.
  * The man's yaw cannot be pinned. His idle routine turns him (LookAround), so a written Yaw is gone within a
    second. What is stable is the yaw he happens to be frozen at, which SimProbe reports. So: freeze first, read the
    drawn yaw, and choose the camera yaw to suit — or, for the Sniper in step 9, refreeze until his drawn yaw is
    within a few degrees of this man's 1.11 rad, and record it here.

ONE FOCUS, ONE ZOOM, ONE PITCH, ONE SIZE — AND A CAMERA YAW PER MAN. Read this before judging the numbers.
  The r1 critique threw r2's IoU out because the two men were framed on two different FOCUS POINTS (50.37 and
  52.09), so the man sat at a different place in the frame and the overlap measured the offset as much as the men.
  That fault is gone: focus is (50, 25) for every still in this set, zoom 30, pitch 22, 1600x900, and the MAN is
  moved to the focus rather than the camera moved to the man. The focus point projects to the same pixels at any
  camera yaw, so both men sit in the same window.
  What could NOT be made equal is the men's own heading. A man at a post is snapped to that POST's facing, and the
  two men do not take the same post at the same point: the prone AT rifleman takes a kind-2 post facing -2.29 rad,
  the kneeling Sniper a kind-1 post facing 2.04 rad. Neither budges — 20 and 26 refreeze cycles were spent trying,
  and a written Yaw is overwritten (see the base set's note). So the heading is a property of the staging, not
  something a camera yaw of 243.6 could be chosen around for both.
  Therefore the camera yaw is turned by exactly the difference between the two men's headings, and nothing else
  is touched:
      AT rifleman   yaw -2.29 rad (-131.21 deg)   camera yaw 243.60
      Sniper        yaw  2.04 rad ( 116.88 deg)   camera yaw 131.69   (= 243.60 + 248.09)
  so cam_yaw - man_yaw is the same for both: each man is seen from the SAME relative angle, side-on, same height,
  same distance, same lens. The two silhouettes are then comparable as silhouettes, which is the claim.
  The cost, said plainly: the GROUND behind the two men is not the same ground, because the camera orbited. That
  does not reach the measure — every mask is a difference against a plate shot from that man's OWN camera, with
  that man and only that man moved off the map — but it does mean the two crops do not look like one place.
