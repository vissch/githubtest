flamer-look-r2 - what each still is, and how it was made (2026-10-19, AFTER the fix)

Scene      GreyboxCorridor, in Play, in the batch-mode editor of the checkout githubtest-relay-work2.
           The scene is already Mood.Night / Biome.NightMud - the owner's target look. No knob was touched.
Spot       x 42, z 12. Map.Height is 2.5 m and level over x 30..48, z 10..16: flat open mud, a bunker behind.
Camera     yaw 90 (TacticalCamera.BaseYaw is -90, so the camera looks along +z, the way these men are drawn:
           we stand behind him, where the fuel tanks are). pose_error_m is 0.000 on all seven stills.
Moment     every still is a frozen world: SimHost.TimeScale = 0 and CaptureRig.Hold(120) - weather clock held at
           120 s, rain 0.20, wind 3.0, identical in all seven. He is stood up in the sim (Stance.Standing, no
           target) and his drawn pose is pinned (Clip.Idle, phase 0.25, no blend, no grime) BEFORE the presenter
           captures the world, because the renderer draws from the presenter's copy and not from the controller.

ONE MAN, ONE SLOT, PHOTOGRAPHED THREE TIMES. The first round of this leg staged three men and the control came
out 829 px different with no kit anywhere: VATRenderer gives each man his own grime seed and character from his
SLOT (seed = slot * 37, chr = Char[slot]) and SimPresenter gives him his own place in the idle loop
(t = frac(AnimTime + i * 0.137f)). So one man is placed, frozen and shot as a Flamethrower; then his archetype is
flipped to Assault in the same slot and he is frozen and shot again; then a third time, the control. Both
archetypes are the same baked soldier, so every pixel that differs between a flamer still and an assault still is
his kit and nothing else.

  flamer_t3.png    T3 (zoom 7.5, pitch 13)  the man as a Flamethrower (archetype 36), from behind.
  flamer_t2.png    T2 (zoom 16,  pitch 22)  the same, the standard fighting view.
  flamer_t1.png    T1 (zoom 30,  pitch 25)  the same, the far view.
  assault_t3/2/1   the same man, same slot, same frozen world, his archetype flipped to Assault: no kit.
  control_t3.png   T3  the Assault a second time, nothing changed between the two: the control.
  *_boxes.png      the same stills with the projected boxes drawn on them (quantised to 128 colours), so a human
                   can see the boxes are on the man: red tanks, orange wand, yellow the whole kit, blue torso,
                   green the ground sample, white cross his feet.

What they show (probe.json, measured off these very files after they were resampled to 1280x720)
  kit_px_t3            2422  (needs >= 150)  the kit is drawn at the near view
  kit_px_t2             574  (needs >= 40)   and at the view the game is played at
  kit_px_control          0  (needs <= 10)   with no kit on the man the measurement reports nothing at all
  tank_vs_torso_dL_t2 31.61 (needs >= 12)  the tanks are that many luma steps LIGHTER than the soldier they are
                      worn over (the torso box of the bare man: the camera is behind him, so his own chest box is
                      the kit). Before the fix this was -6.64: dirt on dirt, the critic's second finding.
  tank_vs_ground_dL_t2 65.53 (needs >= 10) and that much lighter than the mud he stands in.
  (t1 is measured and is not a threshold: 161 px of a 36x15 box differ - the kit still registers at the far view,
  which it did not before, because DrawKit no longer gives up when the near-tier sockets are gone.)

What was changed to get there (all in the SHOW lane)
  CombatFx.cs        a kitMat of its own - pale steel (CombatFx.KitColour) with a strong outline and an emission
                     that keeps it off the mud at night - instead of dirtMat.
  CombatFx.Kit.cs    the tanks hang off the way he FACES (flat forward, world up) and 0.55 m behind the chest
                     socket, clear of the baked soldier's cape; they used to follow the barrel and ended up inside
                     his own body. The kit is sized with VATRenderer.ScaleAt, the man's own size, not ViewNow's
                     old grow-with-zoom scale, which drew it as a 1.5 m slab. And past the near tier the pose is
                     estimated instead of skipped, so he reads as a Flamer at T2 and T1 too.

Reproduce: the phase lines at the top of stage.cs, then `python probe.py --check` (exit 0).
