His heading, which is the other half of the owner's complaint
-------------------------------------------------------------
A man's yaw cannot be written: he snaps to his post's facing, and the previous set paid 1.11 rad for it in every
reading. `AnimationController.cs:462` sets BodyYaw = atan2(step.x, step.z) whenever the shown speed is over 0.15,
and a staging teleport IS a step. So the staging ends with a one-metre hop along the Sniper's own drawn yaw: he is
written a metre back along psi, then onto the target with Speed 1, then left to settle back into ProneIdle with
Speed 0 (a settle write cannot move a heading, because the speed in it is 0). Read back per candidate in
cand-poses.json. It works: nine of the twelve candidates come back at a heading difference of 0.00 to 0.01 rad
instead of 1.11, and the three that do not (cand1 2.86, cand3 1.75, cand11 1.36) are candidates where the man ends
up on a trench post that re-snaps him.

THE WEATHER IN HIS BOX IS CLEARED, AND NOT BY TRADING THE GATE
--------------------------------------------------------------
This is the one thing part 1 sent to the owner, and it is answered. `CaptureRig.Hold` stops the sim and pins the
sky but NOT the night lamps: their clock advances on `Time.unscaledDeltaTime` every frame
(`Presentation/Terrain/NightLights.RealLamps.cs:92`, the same pattern in `Storm.cs:252`), whatever `timeScale` is.
Both lines take `Time.captureDeltaTime` instead when it is positive, so a tiny positive value freezes the fires.
Two further facts had to be measured before that worked:

  * A WRITE MADE WHILE FULLY HELD NEVER REACHES THE PICTURE. With `timeScale` 0 the sim does not step, the
    presenter captures no poses, and a man written off the map is still drawn exactly where he was: measured here
    with three stills, 2 men -> 2 men after the write -> 1 man only after the sim was allowed to step. So the
    plates cannot be shot without live frames in between, which is what cost part 1 its 135-190 frame span.
  * `Time.timeScale` ABOVE 100 IS IGNORED by Unity (assigning 1000 leaves it at its old value). So the staging
    runs at `timeScale` 100 with `captureDeltaTime` 1e-4: 0.01 s of game time a frame, which moves a man in about
    200 frames and costs 0.02 s of lamp time.
  * THE IDLE FRAMES ARE THE MANY. A round spends some 15 000 frames held while the driver polls `Pending()`, and
    the lamp clock advances on every one of them. At `captureDeltaTime` 1e-5 those alone drifted the lamps 0.15 s
    and let 18-66 weather px into his box; at 1e-7 while held, with 1e-4 only during a restaging, round 1 came
    back with `noise_in_box_px_at-1` 0 AND `noise_in_box_px_sniper-*` 0 in all three rounds.

  Measured, with the plates bracketing the three man stills as the plan asks (`empty-{r}` first, `floor-{r}` last,
  so `|empty - floor|` covers the whole span the masks were shot in):

    round   weather frame-wide   in his box   in the kneel's box
      1          69301 px           0 px            0 px
      2          71243              32              0
      3          17280             139              0

  Rounds 2 and 3 still carry 32 and 139 px, from the retries they needed (a retry is more staging frames). The
  technique is right and round 1 proves 0 px is reachable at this camera; it is not yet reliable per round.

WHY IT IS STILL RED: THE KNEELING REFERENCE MAN
------------------------------------------------
The owner's bar compares the prone man against a kneeling man, and the kneeling man has to be a still the capture
itself is willing to measure. Where he stands decides both, and the two cannot be had at once on this ground:

    his place     ground   the rig's readability      his mask      what it costs
    (45, 26)      1.61 m   contrast 0.10, FLAGGED     1245 px       `warnings` non-empty: the set is unfit
    (45, 27)      0.71 m   contrast 0.28, clean        610 px       half-sunk in the hollow
    (44, 27)      0.71 m   contrast 0.80, clean        515 px       half-sunk; what the three rounds used
    (43, 27)      0.72 m   contrast 0.00, FLAGGED      115 px
    (45, 28)      0.71 m   contrast 0.46, clean        433 px
    (45, 29)      0.71 m   contrast 0.04, FLAGGED      152 px

`CaptureRig.Measure`'s readability ring samples the frame 1.3 m above WORLD y = 0, not above the ground the man is
drawn on (the man BOX was fixed for that in d8d91c093e; the ring was not). On the 1.61 m ground at part 1's (45, 26)
that sample lands in the mud under him, so the rig calls its own still "men barely separate from the ground:
unreadable" - 0.10 against the 0.12 it wants - with the man clearly drawn and masking 1245 px. Part 1's single
sniper still read 0.42 at that same spot from the same pixels: the disc is 6 px across, and recomputing the rig's
formula by hand on both PNGs gives 0.19 and 0.21, so the number is a coin flip of where that disc lands, not a
property of the still. Only the low ground at z 27-29 puts the sample on his chest, and there he is half-sunk.

So the three rounds were shot with him at (44, 27), the one spot that is both readable and kneeling (KneelIdle),
and the reading came out:

    silhouette_diff_readings   0.6894 / 0.6025 / 0.6038      worst round 0.6025, at the 0.6 bar
    spread_readings            0.0868
    spread_at                  0.1043                        the prone man is stable
    spread_sniper              0.7303   <-- OVER THE BAR     his mask was 313 / 413 / 505 px

A half-sunk man's own silhouette moves more between rounds (0.73) than the two men differ (0.60), so the measure
cannot carry the claim however the reading falls, and `probe.py` says so: "the spreads reach 0.7303, not under 0.6".
An earlier set with him fully visible at part 1's (45, 26) read 0.37 - the prone body and the kneeling body are
genuinely alike at zoom 30 - and that set is unfit for the other reason (the flagged still).
