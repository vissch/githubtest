Measured by probe.py into probe.json: throw_m 1.712 m (the median of the three blast runs' per-body
distances pooled), per-run medians [1.181, 2.142, 1.212] against the pre build's baseline [1.181, 2.142, 1.212],
noise floor [0.287, 0.076, 0.121] m, px_per_m 32.8. contrast_median is -1 in every sidecar: CaptureRig measures a
man's contrast from his head and foot on screen, and straight down those are the same pixel, so that number cannot
be read from this view. The readability the critic asked for is instead plain in the stills and in the probe finding
six men as an evenly spaced row on a lit, fog-free field.

What the set says, against harsh critic r1's "still in a heap where they stood": every body comes to rest 1.71 m
from its spot (pooled median), 6-22x the 0.08-0.29 m a standing row drifts by on its own, so the heap is measured
away. The frames also match the seconds now: 124 frames for the 4 s pair, not r1's 2984.

What it does NOT say, for the next round: the throw the controller records for these men is 4-10 m out and 3-8 m up
(logged per slot by BlastThrowStills), and the body is drawn to rest a sixth of that. The floor this unit put in
Die (MinThrowOut 2.2, MinThrowUp 1.4) is a real guard for a burst that gives no knock -- BlastReactionTests.
ABlastDeathWithNoKnockIsStillThrownClear -- but it never binds in this scene, where closeness is already high, which
is why the post medians equal the pre ones to the millimetre. The remaining gap between 4-10 m thrown and 1.2-2.1 m
rested is downstream of Die, in VATRenderer.ThrowPath / CombatFx.Deaths (the arc's horizontal reach), and that is
where a round 3 should look.
