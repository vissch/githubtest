water-entry-splash-r6 - what each still is. Facts only; the reading of them is in probe.json and in the commit.

The run. GreyboxCorridor in Play, ground ShelledForest (the scene's own default), NightMud look, rain forced to 0,
sky pinned (Atmosphere.PinnedClock = 120), the water's own sheet pinned (WaterRings.PinnedClock = 120), sim frozen
(SimHost.TimeScale = 0) so no man moves or wades, presentation clock running at Time.captureFramerate = 60 so every
frame is exactly 1/60 s. A body is flung off the dry bank at (12, 128, y 0.69) into the ford at (15, 130), bed
y -0.25, water surface y 0.15 - the two production lines CombatFx.Deaths.cs runs for a flung body,
VATRenderer.AddFallen and WaterEntry.Throw with FallenFlight.Solve's duration as the delay. Staged by
AgentScripts/water_entry_fling.cs in one run.

fly.y is not chosen, it is BISECTED: the harness halves 0.3..0.8 until Solve's duration is 43.00000 frames at 1/60
to within 1e-4 of a frame (fly.y 0.49, flight 0.71667 s). r5 threw with fly.y 0.5, a 43.26-frame flight, and counted
frames forward from each thaw, so every shot missed its moment (+0.033 / +0.283 / -0.217). Here the three freezes
are scheduled on game time from the landing instant, and timings.txt holds what was achieved, measured by the
harness: pre -0.200, land +0.050, post +0.300, land-run2 +0.050. The throw waits 90 settled frames after Play
starts: the first frames of a session still carry the startup's long delta times, and one of them put the pre shot
two frames late in an earlier run.

All ten stills: 1600 x 900, camera T1 (zoom 30, yaw 21, pitch 25), focus (15, 130), aimY 0.15 (the water's
surface). Every sidecar reports pose_error_m 0 and the same mark: mark_x 15, mark_z 130, mark_px_x 800.0,
mark_px_y 450.0, mark_px_per_m 19.222 (CaptureRig.Mark, set before the first shot).

The ford starts with a clean sheet. A man waded it in the seconds of Play before the harness ran, and a wading ring
lives about 1.4 s, so two or three were still open on the water at the first moment shot. They are not the water
entry, but lifting the rings out for a reference lifted them too, and the first r6 run read 791 changed pixels at
land - 0.2 s because of it. The harness now parks every slot once at staging, with the sim already frozen so no new
one can be added, and the entry's own ring is the only ring in any frame here.

Why every moment has its own reference and the plate is only for the record: the water's foam and lapping are
animated from the water clock in TWWater.hlsl, so a still taken a second after the plate differs from it across the
whole water sheet - about 1,600 changed pixels in a 3 m disc at the landing point, whether anything landed there or
not. Measured that way the first run of r5's capture read ring_px_pre 1577 against ring_px_land 1703 and could not
tell a splash from the river moving. A reference rendered at the same frozen instant with the splash lifted out has
the same foam, the same body, the same shadows and the same sky.

Two things seen in these frames that are not the claim under test, recorded because they are visible:
 - The ring STILL does not register on its own at T1, and now there is a number for it rather than prose:
   ring_only_px 0, peak luma move 0.019 against a 0.02 threshold (land against land-noring). r6 widened the ring
   and opened it from a hole instead of a point, which lifted it from 0.014 to 0.019 of luma, but 0.05 s after the
   entry it is still a thin bright line on dark night water, under what a player would notice at this zoom. What
   the water entry shows at T1 is the crown; the ring is detail under it, and it reads later and from closer.
 - The splash cards live 0.7 s, not r5's 0.5 s (WaterEntry.CardLife). At 0.5 s the capture measured 597 changed
   pixels a third of a second after the entry against the owner's bar of 600, and a column of water that is gone in
   half a second looks snatched away rather than fallen back.
