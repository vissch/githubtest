atrifle-look-r3-g1-g1-g2 — the frames that hold both men are the frames that are scored
========================================================================================

Why there is a round g1-g1-g2
-----------------------------
The owner threw out atrifle-look-r3-g1-g1 in his own words: "The pair frames are the only shots with
both men, and they are not the frames the probe scores. pair-3's sniper is phase 0.41 while sniper-3
is 0.80. Score pair-1, pair-2 and pair-3, and put the silhouette bar outside the spread of three
captures of the current build."

He was right, and that set's own poses.json says how far apart the two instants were. It shot five
stills a round and scored two of them: `at-{r}`, with the Sniper written off the map, and
`sniper-{r}`, with the AT rifleman written off. Two stills are two shutter moments, and between them
the driver wrote the world twice and drifted each man to his own band:

    round   scored                      the pair still of the SAME round
    1       AT 0.12   Sniper 0.15       AT 0.80   Sniper 0.18
    2       AT 0.45   Sniper 0.48       AT 0.58   Sniper 0.53
    3       AT 0.73   Sniper 0.80       AT 0.88   Sniper 0.41

So the number was honest about two frames; it was not a reading of one instant of the game. The 0.77
it reported was "a prone man at one moment" against "a kneeling man at another".

What changed in the capture
---------------------------
1. **The solo stills are gone.** Nine stills, not fifteen: `pair-{r}`, `empty-{r}`, `floor-{r}`.
   Nothing scores `at-{r}` or `sniper-{r}` any more, so they are not shot. The reading of a round is
   the AT mask against the Sniper mask cut out of the SAME `pair-{r}` frame: one shutter, one
   instant, both men.

   That costs the measure its easy mask. In a solo still anything in the frame that was not in the
   plate was that one man. Here each mask is confined to the box the capture itself says it drew
   that man in, and the probe fails if the two boxes overlap. The boxes came out 27 px apart
   (AT x 682-787, Sniper x 814-919) and the second-largest blob inside either box was 0 px in all
   six readings, so the Sniper's shadow never reached into the AT man's box on this set. That was
   the risk the move onto one frame carried, and it is measured, not assumed.

2. **The phase search is JOINT.** Both men have to be spaced against themselves in the frames that
   are scored, and one frame holds both, so the shutter moment has to suit both at once. A spell is
   three stepped editor frames (about 0.05 s) with the sim's TimeScale 1 and NOTHING written to the
   world - a staging write is exactly what pinned an earlier round's phases - and both men are read
   in ONE eval per spell, so the two phases are of one moment:

       round 1   shot as soon as both men are in their idle clip. Both phases recorded.
       round 2   the first moment each man is a loop-distance 0.28-0.42 from his OWN round-1 phase.
       round 3   the first moment each man is at least 0.25 from his own round-1 AND round-2 phases.

   At 0.05 s the AT man advances about 0.010 of his ProneIdle loop (4.9 s) and the Sniper about
   0.029 of his KneelIdle loop (1.7 s). Round 1 closed on the first spell, round 2 after 263 spells
   (198 of them skipped, because a `TrenchRoutine` beat was not the idle) and round 3 after 209.
   **Round 3 closed at the strict 0.25 floor; it was not relaxed to 0.22.**

3. **A third mechanical repair, found while shooting this round.** A man fresh off his spawn walk is
   still swinging onto his post's heading when his idle clip has already started. The first capture
   of this set put the AT rifleman at yaw 0.06 in pair-1 and 1.12 in pair-2, and the probe's heading
   gate bit on it - correctly, because a self-spread over two headings measures the turn and not the
   pose. So the driver now runs `settle_heading()` before round 1's search: spells until BOTH yaws
   are unchanged within 0.01 rad over 20 consecutive spells. It settled at AT yaw 1.11 and Sniper
   yaw 0.00, the same headings g1-g1 shot, and after that the staging write does not move them: the
   heading offset against pair-1 is 0.00 rad for both men in all three rounds.

The three repairs from g1-g1 are kept and were not rediscovered: a man parked off the map loses his
Alive flag and comes back DeathThrown, so the staging write ORs it back (`w.Flags[s] |= 1`);
`Pending() == 0` is the queue and not the file, so a shot is waited for on its PNG; and a drift
budget must cover a full loop of the slower idle. One more, from this round's shape: the sim is
FROZEN (TimeScale 0) for the shot itself, so the frames the capture steps while it renders do not
move the pose that was just read.

What this set still does not cover
----------------------------------
* **The parapet halves the prone man.** At yaw 180 the AT rifleman lies behind the sandbags and the
  Sniper kneels clear of them, so the reading is "a prone man half behind bags" against "a kneeling
  man in the open". That is what the game shows from here, and it is why his mask is a quarter the
  size of the Sniper's. A reading of 0.76 is honest for THIS camera; it is not a claim about the two
  models side by side.
* **The rifle is not legible at zoom 30.** The 1.65 m anti-tank rifle is a few dark pixels. No
  number here isolates it, so nothing in this set says the weapon reads.
* **The two men's headings differ by 1.11 rad**, reported as `heading_diff_rad` and NOT gated: one
  camera yaw cannot be side-on to two men whose headings are snapped to their posts, and one frame
  was the owner's call. What IS gated is each man against himself across the three pair stills.
* **One seed, one ground, one time of day.** Seed 12648430, battlefield 1917, ShelledForest, night
  with braziers lit. Nothing here says the number holds on another map or in daylight.
* **Two figures, not two models.** Every infantry archetype except the sniper is drawn as the
  Soldier figure (`VATRenderer.FigureOfArchetype`), so what is measured is the Soldier figure prone
  against the Sniper figure kneeling - a pose-and-figure difference, not a per-unit model.
* **Three poses are three poses, not the clip.** Each man was shot at three spaced phases of ONE
  idle loop. That is a floor for the measure, not a survey of everything he does.

How to re-run it
----------------
    python docs/evidence/atrifle-look-r3-g1-g1-g2/probe.py      # exit 0 on this set

The probe writes probe.json beside itself. It needs numpy, scipy and Pillow and reads nothing but
the nine PNGs, their sidecars and poses.json - no editor. `probe.py <another folder>` measures
another set and prints the report WITHOUT writing probe.json, so a control cannot clobber this
set's number.

Checks
------
Negative control, the gate biting on the set it was written for: the same probe.py run against
atrifle-look-r3-g1-g1's own nine pair-and-plate stills exits 2 and names exactly what the owner
threw out -

    python docs/evidence/atrifle-look-r3-g1-g1-g2/probe.py docs/evidence/atrifle-look-r3-g1-g1

    FAIL: the at's ProneIdle pair phases [0.8, 0.58, 0.88] come within 0.080 of each other, want
          0.2 - this is one pose, not three
    FAIL: the sniper's KneelIdle pair phases [0.18, 0.53, 0.41] come within 0.120 of each other,
          want 0.2 - this is one pose, not three

That run's pixels are fine - it reads 0.7869 / 0.7789 / 0.7785 with the two boxes clear of each
other - and it still fails, because g1-g1's PAIR frames were never driven: the driver spaced the
solo stills and let the pair stills land wherever they landed. The 0.2 gate on the frames that are
scored is the gate that catches that, and it is the one this round had to pass.
