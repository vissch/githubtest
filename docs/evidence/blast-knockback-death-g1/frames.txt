Why the strip had to be reshot for this
  Before this leg a strip still cost only the two frames CaptureRig.Drain waits, so the twelve stills landed
  0.033 s apart and the whole strip spanned 0.4 s: it sat inside the burst's flash and ended while the bodies
  were still in the air, and there was no bounce frame to pick. BlastThrowStills now lets StripGap (7) frames by
  after each still, so they are about 0.15 s apart and the strip spans ~1.8 s, which is what its header always
  claimed. These twelve come from that run.

What probe.py measures, and what the numbers are worth
  A pixel becomes a ground point by intersecting its camera ray with y = ground_y, taken per still from its own
  sidecar: the three spots are not at the same height (ground_y 1.40, 0.93, 1.51 on the stand frames) and the
  greybox is not flat, so one plane for the whole set would be wrong.
  throw_m, seg_air_bounce_m and seg_bounce_settled_m use the old probe's definition
  (docs/evidence/blast-knockback-death/probe.py) so the two sets compare: the centroid of the mass present in the
  earlier still and gone in the later one, against the centroid of the mass present in the later and not in the
  earlier. Pixels brighter than the stand frame's luma_p95 are dropped first, so the flash does not drag the air
  and bounce centroids.
  That mass is the burst's smoke and clods as much as it is the bodies. It shows: the three captures measured
  11.22 / 9.59 / 5.06 m of throw, 1.65 / 4.91 / 1.48 m air-to-bounce and 16.20 / 0.41 / 4.98 m bounce-to-settled.
  The bar rule (below the minimum by at least the spread) therefore gives a negative number for all three, and
  they are clamped to 0: these three numbers are recorded, not guarded. What the probe does hold is the sidecar
  bars (zoom 30, pitch 25, luma_mean <= 0.30, no warning but the expected one, the row standing in `stand` and
  down and drawn in the other three) and the row-spacing self-check. Narrowing them wants a mask that is bodies
  only — the figures are khaki against blue mud, which the row check already uses, but the thrown ones are lit by
  the burst, so that mask is its own piece of work.

The row-spacing self-check
  The standing men in c1_stand are warm (R - B > 4) pieces inside a fixed box (420,180)-(780,420); five of the six
  are separate there, the sixth is behind a bunker at this angle. Each one's lowest pixel unprojects to a ground
  point, and the span from the first to the last over the four gaps comes out at 2.82 m against the staged 3.0 m,
  inside the 0.5 m tolerance. The men land at x 8.24, 10.74, 14.06, 17.38, 19.51 on z ~106, against the staged
  8, 11, 14, 17, 20. The 3 m is given (DeathLab.Row's spacing argument), not worked out here: if this reading
  drifts, the probe's metres mean nothing and it fails rather than returning quiet nonsense.

The one warning the stills carry
  "men barely separate from the ground: unreadable" is CaptureRig's contrast check, and it is expected: a body
  lying in the mud is, by design, barely separate from it. It appears on the frames where the row is down and on
  the stand frames of c2 and c3, which still hold the earlier captures' dead. Any other warning fails the probe.

A thing the next reader should know
  c2 and c3 are shot where an earlier capture's bodies still lie, so their stand frames count 11 and 10 men in
  frame and their `fallen` starts at 6 and 12. The probe asks for men_in_frame >= 6 on a stand frame and
  fallen >= 6 on the others, not a flat 6.
