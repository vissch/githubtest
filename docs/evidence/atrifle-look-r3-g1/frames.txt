ONE CAMERA, ONE FRAME, BOTH MEN                               (2026-10-20, atrifle-look-r3-g1, leg 02)
=====================================================================================================

What r3 did, and why the owner threw it out
-------------------------------------------
r3 shot the AT rifleman and the Sniper at ONE focus point but from TWO camera yaws (243.6 and 131.69), each man
moved onto that focus in turn. The owner's complaint, in his words: "two places", "the sniper still is flagged
unreadable", "the base crop has two men and the base plate still reports a man in the frame, so 0.827 is not one
man against one man".

This set answers all three. ONE camera pose for all twelve stills. The two men stand five metres apart in the SAME
frame at the same moment (pair-1/2/3.png prove it: men_in_frame 2). Each man's mask is |his still - the empty
plate| with every other man off the map, so a mask is one man against nothing: at-r.json says men_in_frame 1,
sniper-r.json says 1, empty-r.json says 0. No sidecar in the set carries a warning, so the "men barely separate
from the ground: unreadable" flag of r2/r3 is gone for BOTH men at this camera.

THE HONEST LIMIT OF ONE CAMERA. The two men's own headings are set by their posts and nothing moves them. At the
moment of pair-1 the AT rifleman is drawn at yaw -2.08 and the Sniper at -1.11: a residual heading difference of
0.97 rad (55.6 deg) that one camera cannot remove, because one camera yaw cannot be side-on to two men facing
different ways. From round 2 on, both men happen to be drawn at yaw 0.79 (their idle beats turned them), so the
difference is 0.00 there; that is luck, not control, and it is recorded per still in poses.json rather than
claimed. This is the cost of the owner's "one frame, one camera": r3 bought an equal relative angle by moving the
camera and paid with two places; this set buys one place and pays with the heading. Neither is hidden.

The frame was cleared
---------------------
One WriteWorlds, sim running, moved every alive non-vehicle slot except those two within 120 m of the men to
(-5000, 0, -5000). It moved 0 men: at this point of this battle nobody else was within 120 m. The clearing is
still what makes men_in_frame trustworthy, and the sidecars prove the result: 2 in every pair, 1 in every single,
0 in every empty plate.

Three rounds, three live poses
------------------------------
Before each round the sim was let RUN for a different spell - 0.4 s, 1.2 s, 2.0 s - and refrozen, so the prone
man's clip phase moves and the set is not one frozen pose three times (r3's three fix masks were 546, 546, 546 px,
exactly identical, because a prone man at a post has no LookAround beat). ProneIdle phase at the AT rifleman's own
still: 0.10 (r1), 0.31 (r2), 0.25 (r3). KneelIdle phase at the Sniper's own still: 0.72, 0.25, 0.69. Every pose is
recorded in poses.json: slot, clip, stance, drawn x/z, drawn yaw and phase, read at the moment of that capture.

The eval lines, verbatim and in order (Tools/tw eval, or `unity cmd eval -- --code '...'`; the full log of every
line and its answer is evals.log in the leg folder, and the driver is rounds.py there):

  UnityEditor.SceneManagement.EditorSceneManager.OpenScene("Assets/_Project/Scenes/GreyboxCorridor.unity"); UnityEditor.EditorApplication.isPlaying = true; return "play";
  return TW.Editor.VfxLab.Rain(0f);
  return TW.Editor.SimProbe.Match();
  return TW.Editor.TankCapture.Spawn(0, 27, 50f, 11f, 21f);      // -> "slot 12"; CHECKED with SimProbe.Unit(12)
  return TW.Editor.TankCapture.Spawn(0, 3, 56f, 25f, 21f);       // -> "slot 0";  CHECKED with SimProbe.Unit(0)
  // six times, the sim RUNNING (the presenter will not move a frozen man), Hp pinned every time:
  var h=UnityEngine.Object.FindFirstObjectByType<TW.Presentation.SimHost>(); h.WriteWorlds(m=>{ m.World.Position[12]=new Unity.Mathematics.float3(50f,0f,25f); m.World.Speed[12]=0f; m.World.Hp[12]=90f; }); return 0;
  // ... the same for slot 0 at (45,25) with Hp 80, then:
  var h=...; h.TimeScale=0f; return "frozen";
  TW.Editor.CaptureRig.Rig.YawPin = 0f; return "yaw pinned to world";
  return TW.Editor.CaptureRig.Hold(120f);
  return TW.Editor.CaptureRig.Shot("<dir>/pair-1.png", 47.5f, 25f, 30f, 180f, 22f, 1600, 900);
  return TW.Editor.CaptureRig.Release();
  // then, for each plate: TimeScale=1, write the man (or both men) to (-5000,0,-5000) with Hp kept ALIVE so he
  // can come back, TimeScale=0, Hold(120), Shot(... at-1 / sniper-1 / empty-1 ...), Release, both men written home.

Hp is pinned in every single write. r3 lost two restages to a staged man being shot dead at (50,25); this set lost
none. A man moved off the map keeps his Hp here (r3 set it to 0): he is then still alive but outside the frame, so
men_in_frame skips him and he can be brought home without restarting Play.

What this set does NOT cover - read before judging any number built on it
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * At yaw 180 the prone AT rifleman lies BEHIND the sandbag parapet and only his back, head and the top of his
    gun clear it, while the kneeling Sniper stands clear of the bags. That is what the game shows from this
    angle, and it is why his mask is the smaller of the two. A silhouette comparison here therefore measures
    "a prone man half behind bags" against "a kneeling man in the open".
  * The two men are at different places in the frame, by design: that was the owner's ask. A raw pixel IoU is
    meaningless between them; any comparison has to align the two masks on their own bounding boxes first.
    Settling that measure is probe.py's job and it is not in this leg (this leg is part 1 of 2).
  * The 1.65 m anti-tank rifle is still not separately legible at T1 (zoom 30): the barrel is a few dark pixels.
    No number in this set isolates it, and no claim about it is made.
