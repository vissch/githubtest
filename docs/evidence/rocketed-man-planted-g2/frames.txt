How they were taken, and the two things that went wrong before they were:
  - the shot is queued by an EditorApplication.update watcher armed BEFORE the shell, and taken one GAME second
    after the man lands. Game time in this batch editor runs several times wall-clock, so the earlier way (poll
    DeathLab.Planted() from outside, then sleep "lands in T, +1 s" on the wall clock) took its stills 20 to 25 game
    seconds late: the legs had already folded (squash -15, feet 0.467 m). Nothing about the look; a harness fault.
  - a fresh Play session and a site of its own per man. Shelling one spot over and over craters the ground and the
    hole floods: one run's stills came back as open water with the man sunk in it (drawn ground -1.2 to -1.5 m,
    CaptureRig warning "unreadable"). A man under water is not evidence either way.
  - T1 standard throughout (zoom 30, yaw +21, pitch 25, 1600x900), aimed at his legs: aimY = man y + PlantDepth
    1.120 + 0.45 (mid-legs), where man y is Path.Rest, his sunk origin.
  - close_1.png is the same man as plant_1 at zoom 7.5, queued in the same frame. It is NOT a plant_*.png, so the
    probe still sees exactly three captures.

THE FINDING: the frozen pose itself is flat.
PlantedPoseTests (new, Assets/_Project/Tests/Show/PlantedPoseTests.cs; EditMode, no GPU, no scene) reads the baked
atlas Resources/Units/FigureSoldier, samples row Clip.DeathThrown at phase 0.02 -- the exact phase VATRenderer
freezes a GagFlags.Freeze gag at -- the way the shader samples it (RowTable, frame floor/ceil, lerp, dequantised
with PosMin/PosSize), then applies the drawn transform (upside down about VatTint.Pivot, squash 0, UnitScale 1.125,
his Pos PlantDepth under the mud) and measures the bounding box of everything above the mud:

    height 0.916 m   width 1.202 m   (x 1.202, z 1.126)   452 of 917 vertices above the mud

The height is right: 0.905 m +- 0.08, the spec (DeathGags.PlantLegsAboveM). The shape is wrong: it is 1.3 times
WIDER than tall, and half his vertices are above the mud, so what stands up is half a splayed body, not a pair of
legs. VATBaker.Throw tips a Thrown-baked clip back to flat over the first third of AnimationController.ThrownLands,
and phase 0.02 is already past upright. The probe's own shape gate (bbox height > bbox width) is therefore failed by
the pose before a single pixel is drawn, which is why every pixel measurement of every still, by every method tried
over two legs, has come back wider than tall.

The probe still FAILS on these three (python docs/evidence/rocketed-man-planted-g2/probe.py, exit 1):
  plant_1.png FAIL area 1 < gate 48
  plant_2.png FAIL bbox height 31 not > width 62
  plant_3.png FAIL area 4 < gate 48
Nothing in probe.py was touched: BAR stays 0.10, the shape gate stays, three captures stay required.

The close still settles it by eye: close_1.png (zoom 7.5, where 0.905 m of legs would be about 85 px) shows a
splayed stub in the mud with blood around it, about as wide as it is tall -- not a pair of legs standing up.

So this is a BUG in the drawn pose, not the owner's look question, and part 2 of the plan fixes that one thing.
PlantedPoseTests carries the measurement: its height assertion is green now, and the shape assertion is written
out in a comment beside it with these numbers, for the fix to add back as a test (a red or [Ignore]d test fails the
gate, so it cannot be left running). See docs/reference/decisions.md, "Answered, and NOT by the owner".

--- the superseded stills of earlier on 2026-11-08, and the 2026-10-27 pair before them ---

Three rocketed men planted head first in the mud (DeathGag.Planted), one man per shell, captured fresh against the
current build in GreyboxCorridor at the standard zoom (T1: zoom 30, yaw +21, pitch 25, 1600x900), 2026-11-08, from
a batch-mode editor driven by one scripted loop (no hand retries). Each still is taken one GAME second after that
man lands: DeathLab.Planted() is polled right after the shell until it answers "lands in T s" with T positive, and
the shot is queued once Time.time has passed landsAt + 1. Each shot is aimed at his legs, not at y 0:
aimY = man y + PlantDepth 1.120 + 0.45 (mid-legs), where man y is Path.Rest, his sunk origin.

These three replace the 2026-10-27 pair. That pair was shot with the default aim (aimY 0.000) while the man lay at
y -1.48, and a sweep of the probe's window row over every value from cy 390 to 510 found no candidate that cleared
the gates on either still at any row -- so the pair could not settle the question whatever the row maths said. The
withdrawal of 2026-10-27 (decisions.md) claimed the two FAILs were only a window-placement bug; that claim is not
supported, and the question is asked again below with the numbers from these three.

What the pixels show, measured in a 120 x 80 px box around the aim point (0-1 Rec.709 luma, the same scale as
CaptureRig's):
  plant_1  mud 0.230   body 166 px, bbox 44 x 20, luma 0.150, contrast 0.320
  plant_2  mud 0.209   body 229 px, bbox 55 x 31, luma 0.104, contrast 0.460
  plant_3  mud 0.226   no red-tunic pixels in the box at all
"body" is the red-tunic mask (r over b by 12/255), segmented independently of the probe.

The finding is NOT that the man is invisible. On two of the three he separates from the mud well over the BAR of
0.10 (contrast 0.320 and 0.460), and CaptureRig's own whole-frame contrast_median is 0.233-0.259 with no
"unreadable" warning -- unlike the 2026-10-27 pair. The finding is that he does not read as PLANTED. The spec is
0.905 m of legs standing above the mud (DeathGags.PlantLegsAboveM), about 21 px tall at this pose; every one of
the three reads as a crumpled body lying roughly flat, two to three times WIDER than tall (44 x 20 and 55 x 31).
That is why the probe's shape gate (bbox height > bbox width) fails and not its contrast bar: there is a man there,
and no legs. A candidate cause, not checked here: the rest fold (RestQ, PlantFoldHeight, "the kick is the roll")
may have already folded the legs down by T+1 s.

Nothing in the probe was loosened to make this pass: BAR stays 0.10, the gates stay, and three captures stay
required. probe.json carries the machine-readable version of the three FAILs.
