Blast knockback death, round 2, leg g1 — 18 stills reshot on a CLEARED field, with the row identified as a lattice.

Why this set was shot twice. The first g1 set (commit 1b560e99a7) was a genuine reshoot, but the instrument was
dirty: every sidecar carried `fallen` 14-22 (old corpses from earlier runs still lay about the field), calm1's -1 s
still carried `drawn_infantry` 10 for a row of six (four living men had wandered into the frame), and the probe
found SEVEN men "standing" in blast1's row of six. From straight above, a corpse already on the ground reads as the
same blob as a man standing, and a corpse does not move when the shell goes off, so each one was a "body" the probe
measured as thrown nowhere. That is this leg's change, and these are the stills taken after it:

  - BlastThrowStills clears every fallen body (VATRenderer.ClearFallen) before each run, and reads the -1 s still's
    own `drawn_infantry` back: a frame holding anything other than the row's six men is retaken on the next
    candidate patch (up to three, each outside the others' 34 m x 22 m frame). All six runs passed on attempt 1.
  - probe.py identifies the row as the scenario's OWN geometry: the best fit of a six-point lattice of SPACING_M
    (`MEN` = 6 and `SPACING_M` = 2.5, both from BlastThrowStills), at most five gaps wide, nearest blob per lattice
    point. Only lattice members are measured, and a run raises if fewer than MIN_LATTICE = 5 of the six fit. A
    stray corpse or a wanderer off the lattice can no longer enter the measured set, however blob-like it is.
  - Instrument, before -> after: `fallen` at -1 s 14-22 -> 0-1 on all six runs; `drawn_infantry` at -1 s 6-10 -> 6
    on all six runs; `fallen` at +3 s on the blast runs 7/13/21 -> 7/6/7 (six men die, one body from the shell's
    own edge). n_standing 7/6/6 -> 6/6/5.

view; the readability the critic asked for is plain in the stills (a lit, fog-free field) and in the probe fitting

module docstring): MAX_BLOB 2000, AREA_W 10, AREA_RATIO 0.1-3.0 as a backstop only, MAX_MATCH_M 12, MIN_BODIES 4.

What the set says. The dirty instrument was not the whole story: on a cleared field, with only lattice men measured,
this build still leaves men near where they stood. blast1's nearest body moved 0.056 m (~2 px) while the controller
recorded a throw of -5.66 m out and 4.51 m up for that slot, and two more of its bodies (0.357 m, 0.524 m) plus
blast3's nearest (0.847 m) are inside the calm spread. blast2, the same scenario on another patch, threw all six
bodies 1.47-4.11 m. So the throw is recorded and sometimes drawn, and sometimes it is not drawn at all — the probe
exits 1 and names the four failing (run, body, distance) pairs.

What it does NOT say: why. The per-slot throws the controller recorded are in the capture log (all 18 blast slots
have one: 3.98-10.23 m out, 3.17-8.17 m up, every ThrowZ 0.00). The next leg starts there — a recorded throw of
5.66 m that moves a body 0.056 m is a presentation defect between the death record and VATRenderer's drawn rest
position (ThrowPath / FallenFlight.Plan.Rest / the heap's EndAt), not a missing throw. Note: every ThrowZ is
exactly 0.00 because the row stands along x and the shell bursts on the row's own line, so the whole throw is in x.
