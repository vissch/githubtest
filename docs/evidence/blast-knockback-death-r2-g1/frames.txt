blast-knockback-death-r2-g1 — the stills, and what they are
===========================================================

Shot by `Assets/_Project/Tests/Stills/BlastThrowStills.cs`
(`BlastThrowsArePhotographedFromAbove`, [Explicit]), one run, 2026-10-24, on commit 4fb78c36c3's tree plus this
leg's capture fix. Measured by `probe.py` in this folder.

Six runs on open ground. Each run stands a row of SIX men 2.5 m apart ACROSS the throw (along z,
`DeathLab.Row(..., alongZ: true)`) and photographs it straight down at three moments around the instant a shell
bursts at the row's middle: -1 s (the standing row), +0.5 s (mid-flight) and +3 s (where the bodies came to rest).
`blast1..3` get the shell (radius 10 m, 600 damage: every man of the row is inside 0.8 * radius, the throw gate in
`AnimationController.Death.cs:209`, and dies outright on 100 hp). `calm1..3` get nothing, and so give the bar its
noise floor: how far a standing column drifts off its own line on its own.

The row stands across the throw because every man in one burst is thrown the SAME way (the burst's own `simDir`,
`AnimationController.Death.cs:219`). This build's recorded throws confirm it: all eighteen read `throw x 0.00`, with
`throw z` from 4.12 to 11.85 m. So the whole throw lands in x, every standing man shares one x, and `probe.py`'s
`across()` can measure `|body.x - row_x|` with no assignment between the before and after blobs at all.

(below).

The pinned camera, read off the sidecars (not typed)
----------------------------------------------------
All eighteen: `zoom` 20, `pitch` 89, `yaw` 0, `cam_y` 52.07724, `cam_pitch` 89.00005, `cam_yaw` 0,
`pose_error_m` 0. Straight down, so world x and z map onto screen pixels at one scale with no foreshortening, and
`px_per_m` comes from the camera's own pose, not from the row: 360 / (20 * tan 30 deg) = 31.18 px/m.
`cam_x`/`cam_z` differ per run (different patches) and `probe.py`'s `to_world` folds them into one frame.
`drawn_infantry` is 6 in every -1 s still: exactly the row, nothing else in the frame.
`contrast_median` of the -1 s stills runs 0.154 (blast1) to 0.534 (calm3).

`frame` gaps are 17 frames for the -1 s to +0.5 s step and 94 for -1 s to +3 s, not the 45 and 120 that
`Time.captureDeltaTime = 1/30` is supposed to give: in batch mode the game clock is not pinned to the frame the way
it is in a windowed editor. The seconds are right (the shots are taken on `Time.time`, which is what the moments are
measured in); only the frame counter is not 30 a second. `probe.py` reports `frame_gap` and `fps_implied` and bars
nothing on them.

The capture fix: why this set could be shot at all
--------------------------------------------------
The previous leg could not get a set: the -1 s still of a fresh, full-hp, correctly-positioned row came back with
`drawn_infantry` 0, intermittently, seven runs in a row. Three faults, all found and fixed this leg; every run of
the green capture passed its clean-frame check on the FIRST take.

1. **A stale presenter.** `SimPresenter.Capture` sets `count = w.HighWater` and runs only after a stepped tick.
   Until it has, `Drawn(slot)` is `float3.zero` for a freshly spawned slot and `PoseCount` is 0, so the VAT fill job
   (`VATRenderer.cs:262`, over `PoseCount`/`PoseSlot`) and `CaptureRig.Measure` (`CaptureRig.cs:270`, over `Drawn`)
   both see the row standing at the origin while `world.Position` is perfectly right: `drawn_infantry` 0 over a
   correct row. The harness now waits (up to 300 frames, no `Hold`, so ticks can step) until `PoseCount > 0` and
   every row slot's `Drawn` is within 0.5 m of its `Position`. It caught up in 1 or 2 frames every time.
2. **A stale sidecar on a retake.** `Drain` waited for the png to exist, but a retake writes over a png that was
   already there, so it returned at once and `Side()` read the take before it. `Forget(path)` deletes the png and
   its sidecar before each take, and `Drain` now waits for both.
3. **The match's own infantry in the frame.** `drawn_infantry` counts every foot figure the game drew, not just the
   row's, so the player's own squads standing near a patch read as men of the row — 11 for a row of six on the
   first patch tried. The "exactly six men" rule then rejected patch after patch until the map ran out of open
   ground (only 6 of 18 stills were written). Every other living foot figure is now moved off-map before the row is
   spawned (moved, never despawned: a freed slot came back reading 0 on the next spawn at it), and
   `VATRenderer.ClearFallen()` clears the bodies. That is also what makes a patch safe to reuse.

What `probe.py` still cannot measure
------------------------------------
The set is good; the probe cannot read all of it yet, so there is no verdict in this file and no `probe.json`.
`probe.py` exits 1 on `blast2`: the row lattice fits a 49.8 px gap (`row_px_per_m` 19.95) where the camera says one
2.5 m gap is 77.94 px, because its gap window (`GAP_MIN_PX`, `GAP_MAX_PX` = 50, 110) is wide enough to admit a
false fit and ties on member count are broken arbitrarily. Anchoring the window to the camera scale clears blast2
but not the rest: in `calm1` and `calm2` the mask finds only 4 of the six men, under `MIN_LATTICE` 5, and the fitted
`row_px_per_m` still spreads 26.2 to 37.1 against the camera's 31.18. The -1 s frames carry 35 to 55 blobs where six
men stand, so the mask, not the capture, is what has to improve next.

Nothing here was tuned to move a verdict: `calm_spread_m` (the largest `across_m` over calm1..3) and
`worst_across_m` (the smallest over blast1..3) are both measured from the pixels of the run being judged, which is
what the owner asked for — "outside the spread of three captures of the current build".
