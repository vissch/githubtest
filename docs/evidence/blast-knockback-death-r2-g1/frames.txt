blast-knockback-death-r2-g1 — the stills, and what they are
===========================================================

Shot by `Assets/_Project/Tests/Stills/BlastThrowStills.cs`
(`BlastThrowsArePhotographedFromAbove`, [Explicit]), one run, 2026-10-24, on commit 4fb78c36c3's tree plus this
leg's capture fix, reshot with the shell off the row line. Measured by `probe.py` in this folder.

Six runs on open ground. Each run stands a row of SIX men 2.5 m apart ALONG z (`DeathLab.Row(..., alongZ: true)`)
and photographs it straight down at three moments around the instant a shell bursts 5 m to -x of the row line
(`ShellOffsetX` in the harness): -1 s (the standing row), +0.5 s (mid-flight) and +3 s (where the bodies came to
rest). `blast1..3` get the shell (radius 10 m, 600 damage: every man of the row is inside 0.8 * radius, the throw
gate in `AnimationController.Death.cs:209`, and dies outright on 100 hp). `calm1..3` get nothing, and so give the
bar its noise floor: how far a standing row drifts on its own.

The shell sits OFF the row line because every man in one burst is thrown straight away from the burst point
(`Explosion.Dir` is zero -- `DeathLab.Shell` takes a point only -- so `AnimationController.Death.cs:219` throws him
along `position - burst`). With the shell on the row's own middle, as the first g1 cut had it, the six men flew
along their own line and the crater covered the very ground the measurement needs. Off the line, every recorded
throw carries a large +x part: 2.1 to 7.4 m across the eighteen deaths, which the test itself asserts
(`ThrowX > 0` per man, after all 18 stills are on disk). The end men stand 6.25 m up the row from the shell's z, so
their direction is 5 : 6.25 and the z part is still the larger one -- `|ThrowX| > |ThrowZ|` is NOT true and is not
asserted.

Each has a `.json` sidecar beside it, written by `CaptureRig`. Each calm run reuses the patch of the blast run with its

patch to find — every living man on the field is moved off it and every fallen body cleared before a row is spawned
(below).

The pinned camera, read off the sidecars (not typed)
----------------------------------------------------
All eighteen: `zoom` 20, `pitch` 89, `yaw` 0, `cam_y` 52.07724, `cam_pitch` 89.00005, `cam_yaw` 0,
`pose_error_m` 0. Straight down, so world x and z map onto screen pixels at one scale with no foreshortening, and
`px_per_m` comes from the camera's own pose, not from the row: 360 / (20 * tan 30 deg) = 31.18 px/m.
`cam_x`/`cam_z` differ per run (different patches) and `probe.py`'s `to_world` folds them into one frame.
`drawn_infantry` is 6 in every -1 s still: exactly the row, nothing else in the frame.
`contrast_median` of the -1 s stills runs 0.154 (blast1) to 0.394 (calm3).

`frame` gaps are 94 frames for the -1 s to +3 s step, not the 45 and 120 that
`Time.captureDeltaTime = 1/30` is supposed to give: in batch mode the game clock is not pinned to the frame the way
it is in a windowed editor. The seconds are right (the shots are taken on `Time.time`, which is what the moments are
measured in); only the frame counter is not 30 a second. `probe.py` reports `frame_gap` and `fps_implied` and bars
nothing on them.

The capture fix: why this set could be shot at all
--------------------------------------------------
The previous leg could not get a set: the -1 s still of a fresh, full-hp, correctly-positioned row came back with
`drawn_infantry` 0, intermittently, seven runs in a row. Three faults, all found and fixed this leg; every run of
the green capture passed its clean-frame check on the FIRST take.

1. **A stale presenter.** `SimPresenter.Capture` sets `count = w.HighWater` and runs only after a stepped tick.
   Until it has, `Drawn(slot)` is `float3.zero` for a freshly spawned slot and `PoseCount` is 0, so the VAT fill job
   (`VATRenderer.cs:262`, over `PoseCount`/`PoseSlot`) and `CaptureRig.Measure` (`CaptureRig.cs:270`, over `Drawn`)
   both see the row standing at the origin while `world.Position` is perfectly right: `drawn_infantry` 0 over a
   correct row. The harness now waits (up to 300 frames, no `Hold`, so ticks can step) until `PoseCount > 0` and
   every row slot's `Drawn` is within 0.5 m of its `Position`. It caught up in 1 or 2 frames every time.
2. **A stale sidecar on a retake.** `Drain` waited for the png to exist, but a retake writes over a png that was
   already there, so it returned at once and `Side()` read the take before it. `Forget(path)` deletes the png and
   its sidecar before each take, and `Drain` now waits for both.
3. **The match's own infantry in the frame.** `drawn_infantry` counts every foot figure the game drew, not just the
   row's, so the player's own squads standing near a patch read as men of the row — 11 for a row of six on the
   first patch tried. The "exactly six men" rule then rejected patch after patch until the map ran out of open
   ground (only 6 of 18 stills were written). Every other living foot figure is now moved off-map before the row is
   spawned (moved, never despawned: a freed slot came back reading 0 on the next spawn at it), and
   `VATRenderer.ClearFallen()` clears the bodies. That is also what makes a patch safe to reuse.

fragment at 120 px, so only the ceiling bites; the crater and the scorch read 14 100 to 63 800 px, an order of
magnitude out.

The capture and the camera are sound (one pinned pose, `pose_error_m` 0, every run's row-measured scale now within
3.5% of the camera's 31.18 px/m, where the first cut spread 26.2 to 37.1). The geometry is sound: the medians are
1.6 to 2.4 m against a calm median of 0.07 to 0.38 m, so the throw IS there and is an order of magnitude over the
calm centre. What fails is the bar's shape, at both ends, and both are mask noise, not knockback:
  * each +3 s frame carries 38-57 candidate blobs in the man-area window -- blood, debris and snow churn. One of
    them lands on a dead man's own standing spot in blast1 and blast3, so his `d_i` reads 0.022 m and 0.000 m
    however far the body itself flew.
  * at the other end, two calm3 men have NO candidate within 1.1 m at +3 s (the mask loses them), so the calm
    spread, defined as the largest `d_i` over the calm runs, reads 1.678 m -- a missing man, not a drifting one.
The two faults meet in the middle, which is why "every blast body clears the calm spread" is red while every
median is wide clear. The bar needs a measurement that cannot be set by a single stray blob (see the leg note:
the `d_i` of a MISSING candidate and of a body ON its spot are the same number today).

Nothing here was tuned to move a verdict: both sides of the bar are measured from the pixels of the runs being
judged, which is what the owner asked for -- "outside the spread of three captures of the current build".

The bare-ground control (added 2026-10-24, leg g1-02, with a fresh 24-still set)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every run now carries a fourth still, `g1_<run>_b0_0.png`: the run's own patch, from the run's own pose, with
nobody standing on it. It is shot after ClearFallen() and the off-map sweep have settled and before the row is
spawned, and the harness fails the run unless its sidecar reads drawn_infantry 0 (all six read 0 on this set; one
pinned camera throughout, zoom 20, pitch 89.00005, yaw 0, cam_y 52.077, pose_error_m 0).

Why: the clutter the probe cannot tell from a body is terrain, not blast churn -- the calm -1 s frames hold 36-48
man-sized blobs with no shell fired at all, and at that density the nearest blob to any point sits 1-2 m away,
which is the size of the throw being measured. No area window, merge radius or constant separates a clod from a
corpse. A still of the same ground with nobody on it does: a blob that is already in the control is ground.
Dead end, not to be retried: "candidates = blobs at +3 s that were not at -1 s" deletes the calm standing men too,
because the calm signal IS that the man is still where he stood.

What the control removes, counted on this set (probe.py's own mask and merge, a +3 s blob dropped when a control
blob sits within 0.6 m of it):

Two cautions for whoever rewrites the bar on it. Three control stills carry fallen 2, 2 and 3 in their sidecars
(blast1, blast3, calm2): ClearFallen() does not empty every body before the control is shot, so a corpse from an
earlier attempt can be IN the control and would then be subtracted away -- they are not on the row's spots here,
but check. And calm1's +3 s sidecar reads drawn_infantry 7 for a row of six: one wanderer walked into that frame
after the -1 s guard had passed.

The fresh set measures the same build as the one before it: probe.py (unchanged this leg) gives blast medians
1.739 / 2.527 / 1.638 m against calm 0.369 / 0.060 / 0.201 m, calm_spread_m 1.678, worst_throw_m 0.0 -- within
0.14 m of the previous capture everywhere. The instrument is repeatable; it is still RED for the two reasons in
the section above, and the control is the material for fixing it.
