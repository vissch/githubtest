(Bin unchanged for blast1, blast2, calm1, calm2, calm3 and five of six of calm1's/calm2's/calm3's three moments) --
only 3 of 18 blast/calm stills in r2 were ever actually reshot after the throw floor landed (AnimationController.
Death.cs:224, MinThrowOut/MinThrowUp). That is why r2's `blast_median_m` equalled its own `BASELINE_THROW_M`
to the millimetre, and why r2's reported blast3 nearest-body distance (0.124 m) proved nothing about the current
build. This set (`g1`) is 18 fresh stills shot on this tree, tag `g1`, one pinned camera, six patches of ground.

stands on its own patch of open ground (BlastThrowStills' 34 m x 22 m spacing). 1280x720, Winter day field, rain
and squalls off, weather clock pinned at 30, game clock frozen for the shot (CaptureRig.Hold), Time.captureDeltaTime

Measured by probe.py into probe.json: throw_m 1.741 m (median of the three blast runs' per-body distances,
pooled), per-run medians [1.102, 2.267, 1.45], noise floor (per-run median) [0.285, 0.047, 0.12] m,
calm_spread_m 0.906 m (the largest single body move across all three calm runs -- the bar), worst_body_m 0.118 m
(the smallest single body move across all three blast runs). px_per_m 31.18, read from the camera's pose, checked
against each run's own row spacing (29.8-33.3 px/m, within the 12% tolerance). contrast_median is -1 in every
sidecar, same as r2: straight down, a man's head and foot are the same pixel, so CaptureRig cannot read it from
this view; the readability the critic asked for is plain in the stills (a lit, fog-free field) and in the probe
finding six men as an evenly spaced row.

Tuning changed from r2's probe.py, against THIS set's own pixels (not against a target result -- see probe.py's
module docstring and its two tuning comments for the full account):
  - MAX_BLOB 1600 -> 2000: a standing man's idle pose can read up to ~1900 px in the mask on this build (measured
    on calm2's third man), and excluding that blob left the matcher no honest candidate near his own spot.
  - AREA_W 40 -> 10: every real match on this set has an area ratio of 0.13-1.2 (a lying body reads far smaller
    than a standing one, seen almost end-on at pitch 89 vs full-length from above); a weight of 40 made matching a
    man to his own true landing spot cost MORE than matching him to a same-sized blob metres away, which is what
    produced r2's and this set's own calm2 "2.6 m move" before the retune (below).
  - The leg card's area-ratio hard filter (drop a match outside 0.5..2.0) could not be implemented at that bound:
    real blast throws measured on this build have ratios as low as 0.129 (a lying man reads an eighth the area of
    a standing one), which overlaps completely with the one bad calm2 pairing it was meant to catch (ratio 0.452).
    No area-ratio bound can separate them -- the calm2 defect is fixed by AREA_W and MAX_BLOB instead (above), and
    the filter is kept at 0.1..3.0 as a backstop that catches nothing seen in this data, not as a working
    discriminator. Before the AREA_W/MAX_BLOB retune, g1's own calm2 showed the same symptom r2 named ("2.433 m",
    cf. git log): a standing man's area, not the row's position, had changed in the +3 s still, and the old weight
    read that as a 2.6 m move.

What the set says: this build's blast3 (0.118 m) and two of blast1's seven bodies (0.357 m, 0.524 m) do not clear
this build's own calm spread (0.906 m). r2's claim that the floor change fixed the near-zero-move body was
unverifiable (its "post" stills were its own "pre" stills); on a genuine reshoot of the same scenario, the same
symptom is there. The probe exits 1 and names the three failing (run, body, distance) pairs; `note.md` in this
leg's folder hands the finding and the measured numbers to the leg that changes the code (plan step 7 / round 3).

What it does NOT say: why. BlastThrowStills.cs (unchanged) logs each dying slot's recorded ThrowX/ThrowZ/ThrowUp
and the controller's own death record per slot to the test log; the next leg should start there, in
VATRenderer.ThrowPath / CombatFx.Deaths (the arc's horizontal reach), same as r2 pointed at.
