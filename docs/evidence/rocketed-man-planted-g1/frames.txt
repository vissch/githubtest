Six rocketed men planted head first in the mud (DeathGag.Planted), one man per shell, in the battle scene
GreyboxCorridor at the standard zoom (T1: zoom 30, yaw +21, pitch 25, 1600x900). Each is taken about a second
after that man lands, while his legs are still kicking (the kick runs three seconds, then they fold). Each shot
is aimed at ground level under the man (aimY = RenderGround.Sample at his resting x z), so the frame centre is
where his head went in and his legs stand 0.905 m above it. The clock was paused and stepped (Tools/tw pause
true, stepto) between staging and the shot, so game time stood still while each was framed.

`base_1..3.png`: the current build, this unit's own un-curated captures (`DeathLab.Scene("shell", x, z)` at a
spread of x z picked before any still was looked at, not after), proving what "a player does not see him" looks
like today. `plant_1..3.png`: the same build, same zoom/yaw/pitch, but staged so his legs land somewhere they
read: open ground, not a flooded crater (`DeathLab.Shell`'s crater floods the middle of its own row — several
trial stagings in this unit landed the body underwater at y -1 to -3 m and were thrown out), and close enough to
a lit patch of the map (a lamp, a fire, or a lit building's painted floor already in the night scene — nothing
added, nothing recoloured) that his dark trousers and boots stand off it, the way plant_1 in the prior unit stood
off its sandbag wall and plant_2 in the same unit did not off bare mud. No camera parameter differs from the
baseline: zoom, yaw and pitch are the T1 standard throughout, only WHERE he is framed changes.

Trial stagings that were tried and thrown out (kept here so the next unit does not re-walk them): x near 89.5,
z in the 120-135 range sits over a flooded crater (y -1.5 to -2.7 m); a body that lands flush against a bright
prop (a crate, a sandbag's metal rim, a wire trestle's cross) can swing a disc sample onto the PROP instead of
the man — several early captures measured a tank-tread or a sandbag wall, not a leg, and were caught by this
unit's column guard (a man's own column is darker than the ring around him on most of its rows; a prop lit from
one side usually is not) and dropped before being used as evidence.
