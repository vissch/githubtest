# Look-deep, 2026-10-01: what the instruments were saying, and what they say now

A day spent on the look and animation work found nothing wrong with the look or the animation. It found that
three of the tools which judge them could not have told us if there had been. This is what changed, what is now
measurable that was not, and the three questions left open with their evidence.

Lane: `lane/show/look-deep-2026-10-01`, based on the plan branch. Every commit gated green on its own.

## What was wrong with the instruments

**The readability number counted machines as men.** `Editor/CaptureRig.cs` measures every drawn man as a disc at
his chest against a median ring of the ground behind him, and warns under 0.12. Its loop filtered on `Alive`
alone, so a walker counted as a man and was measured with a man's geometry — a ring sized to a 2.6 m figure, which
a walker is wider than, so its own hull became the "ground behind him". Eighteen of thirty-six walker stills
counted men while drawing no infantry at all. Machines also score *well*, up to 0.68, so wherever one shared a
frame with men it pulled the median up: excluding them moved those shots from 0.2027 to 0.1225 and took the real
unreadable count from 3 of 18 to 8 of 18.

**And it threw away which way a man failed.** The measure was `|man − bg|`, so "unreadable" could not distinguish
a man too dark for his ground from one too bright — and the two want opposite fixes. `CaptureRig.SignedContrast`
keeps the sign; the sidecar now carries `contrast_signed_median`, `man_luma_median`, `bg_luma_median` and
`men_darker_frac`. The magnitude is unchanged, so every number recorded before today stays comparable.

**The gym's Units tab could not fail.** Every entry declares that the unit fires. `Editor/Gym.cs`'s judge tested
only that it was still alive four seconds after spawning, and the staging gave it no enemy, no reachable range
and a four-second window — so eight of fourteen entries recorded no sim events at all and the tab reported none
flagged. It now stages a mark with `HoldFire` at a range the unit can use, read from the match's own tables, down
its barrel, and checks that something fired. The tab went 0 → 11 → 1 flagged, with 128 rounds behind it where
there had been none. The remaining flag is the officer, who does not survive his own window; see the coupling
below before chasing him.

**The mid figure had never been photographed.** A man is drawn with his full mesh inside 75 m, his mid mesh out to
170 m and the box soldier beyond. The gym's bands ran 30 then 120 — inside 75 m, then past 170 m — so a whole LOD
tier with its own bake was invisible to every capture the game takes, and nothing reported `DrawnMid` either.
Band `M60` and `drawn_mid` fix both.

**The behaviour bench printed a number it could not interpret.** `Tests/EditMode/BehaviourBenchTests.cs` gave
metres driven per machine type with no lifetime beside it, and samples only living units — so a machine destroyed
in ten seconds and one that stood still for eight minutes printed the same small number.

## What is measurable now that was not

| | |
|---|---|
| which way a man fails his ground | `contrast_signed_median`, `men_darker_frac` |
| whether a shot's contrast is a sample or a rate | a warning under three men measured |
| whether a staged unit actually fired, and why not | the Units tab's flag says "it had a target" or "it never acquired one" |
| whether the mid figure is reached | `drawn_mid`, and band `M60` |
| whether a machine's metres mean anything | seconds alive per type, and the shortest-lived machine with its team |
| the look of a unit that is behaving | `keep=1` keeps a clean entry's full-resolution captures |

## Three questions left open, with their evidence

**Men read by value, and value is not the lever.** On a properly staged man the signed contrast is −0.391 close
and −0.147 at mid: he is darker than his ground, as the notes have always said (the walker stills said the
opposite only because they were measuring machines). But the picture says what the number cannot — at the mid band
a man is *readable but not distinguishable*: the field is full of dark vertical things, stumps and sandbags and
pickets, and a man is one more. What finds him is his muzzle flash. A fix should make a man distinct in **kind**,
not in value — and note that this measure is luminance-only, so a khaki man on blue mud at equal luma scores zero
and is perfectly visible. This is why the range lift of 2026-09-27 made things worse rather than by bad luck.

**The Pincer dies in six to thirteen seconds, on the winning side, every seed.** Every other single machine
survives the full ~220 s match. Worth checking whether it is put down inside the enemy's fire, whether its sponson
arcs leave the dead astern its own comment describes, or whether going first is simply what the scripted enemy
does.

**The facing fix and its measure are on sibling lanes.** The fix that stops a man turning about for a one-tick
shove is here; the gym facing measure that justified it is on `lane/show/gym-flatten`, which is not an ancestor of
this lane. They should land together, or the fix is unverifiable and a regression in it is invisible.

## Two things that are correct and look wrong

The mid mesh reports the same vertex count as the full figure: by design, it is the full vertex buffer with fewer
triangles so it can share the figure's atlas. A consequence worth knowing is that `VerticesThisFrame`, and the
shadow budget computed from it, cannot see the mid tier's saving at all.

The gym's Events tab has no case in the judge. Also correct: of its 68 entries, 28 are `Covered`, 34 `Preview` and
6 `Excluded` — not one claims to fire, so there is nothing to check.

## How a clean zero lies

Three different runs reported no failures while examining nothing or the wrong thing: a tab whose only assertion
was "is it alive"; `filter=` given a comma-separated list, which it does not parse, matching zero entries; and
`max=24`, which stopped before the action clips, so twenty-four idle poses passed for "the troop animations are
fine". The count of entries that ran is the only thing that separates these from a real pass.

## Tried and rejected: choosing the mark's bearing by line of sight

The Units tab stands its mark 14 m off, down the subject's barrel. That distance is a margin, not a guarantee —
at 24 m the MG and the officer lost their sightline across a cratered stage — so the obvious improvement is to ask
the terrain which bearing is clear and use that: `HeightfieldRaycast.HasLineOfSight` from chest height over a fan
of 0, ±8, ±16 degrees, kept inside the narrowest gun arc in the game.

It works, and it is still the wrong thing. At 24 m with the fan the tab reads 0 of 14, where the same 24 m without
it left two units silent — so the sightline problem really is solved at the root. But two measurements argue
against keeping it:

- **The margins collapse.** At 24 m with the fan the MG fires once and the officer once, against 46 and 12 at
  14 m, and the tab totals 61 rounds against 128. The fan fixes where the mark stands; it cannot buy back time in
  an eight-second window for a unit that acquires late, and a tab that passes by a single event will flake.
- **A clear line is mutual, and clear lines lead to the battle.** With the fan at 14 m the officer is killed
  before the window ends — reproducibly, the same entry in two identical runs. The gym stages entries inside a
  live match, so a bearing chosen for being unobstructed can be the bearing that gives the real enemy a clear shot
  at the subject. "Not alive 4 s after spawning" then reads as a unit problem when it is a staging one.

At 14 m the direct bearing is almost always clear, so the fan seldom acts — and when it acted it made things
worse. Reverted. If the stage or `MarkNear` ever changes enough that marks start hiding again, the fan is the
right shape of answer, but it needs a rule that prefers a bearing away from the live fight rather than merely an
open one.

## The thresholds, measured

Two instruments judge a look change on this project, and neither had a published floor. Both were measured on
2026-10-01 by running identical code twice.

**`CaptureRig` stills — `contrast_median`.** Paired per shot over 36 walker stills: median |delta| **0.0007**,
p90 0.0140, max 0.0225. The same 14 shots were flagged in both runs, `men_in_frame` was identical in all 36, and
rain and wind were bit-identical (the pinned clock holds). Compare runs **per shot, paired**; the aggregate median
is too coarse. Note the tail: three shots swung up to 0.232 between identical runs, all of them shots where only
ONE man was measured, which is a sample and not a median.

**`Tools/abtest.py gym` — the share of pixels changed.** Across 30 entry-and-band comparisons of identical code:
**16 of 30 bit-identical**, median **0.0000**, p90 **0.0009**, max **0.0012**. The run is paced in game time, and
it shows: the gym's visual output is effectively deterministic, so a change touching more than about 0.2 % of
pixels is real. Run it with `--noise` to re-measure this on any machine before trusting a small difference.

A number below these floors is not a small effect; it is no effect.

## What the behaviour bench reads on an unchanged tree

`Report_HowEveryUnitBehaves` is `[Explicit]`, so the gate skips it and its numbers go unrecorded. Three
eight-minute matches on this lane, 2026-10-01, both seats scripted and fielding machines:

| | seed 1 | seed 2 | seed 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| winner, and when | 0 at 232 s | 0 at 222 s | 0 at 217 s |
| machines / metres | 21 / 3758 | 21 / 3884 | 21 / 3758 |
| shortest life | Pincer (team 0) 13 s | Pincer (team 0) 6 s | Skimmer (team 1) 21 s |
| jammed, spinning | 3 s, 43 s | 8 s, 51 s | 4 s, 45 s |
| flips per 100 m (worst) | 13.3 (Kettle 20) | 15.8 (Pavise 51) | 15.5 (Croaker 45) |
| deaths, biggest clump | 34, 3 | 37, 2 | 36, 3 |

The men are in good order throughout: stuck 0.00 s per man-minute, nothing idle in the open under fire, nothing
piled, 45-50 % of their time in the open of which most is moving, under 0.3 % forced prone and effectively no
pinning. No deaths at all fall in clumps of four or more.

Three things in that table are worth somebody's attention, and none of them were changed here. Team 0 wins all
three seeds, each time in under four minutes of an eight-minute match. Twenty-one machines spend 43-51 seconds
of each match spinning, and individual machines reach 45-51 direction flips per 100 m against a 13-16 average.
And the Pincer dies first on the winning side in two seeds out of three, inside thirteen seconds.

## Gym entries are not independent

They run one after another in a single Play session, and the live battle round the stage keeps advancing between
them. So the number of capture bands is part of every later entry's conditions: adding `M60` lengthened each
earlier entry's photography, and the Units tab moved from 0 flagged with 119 rounds to 1 flagged with 128 —
deterministically, the same numbers on repeated runs, not variance.

Two things follow. A flagged entry late in a run may be reporting a drifted world rather than its own behaviour,
so check whether a change to the capture set preceded it before investigating the unit. And the officer's flag,
"it did not survive its own window", is of that kind: he is killed by a battle he is not part of, in a world that
the seventh band shifted him into.

That flag was also measured in the wrong place until now — after all the photography rather than at the end of the
entry's window — so it used to mean "survived the window and the captures", and its message still said "4 s" long
after the window became eight.
