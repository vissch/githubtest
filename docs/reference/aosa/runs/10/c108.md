# C108: where the night column's orange arcs come from (cycle 10)

Runs: barrage, ShelledForest night. The 64-frame runs start at tick 140 (k0 = default, ka = fx.columnBurstLit=0.3,
kb = fx.columnBurstLit=0.3 with fx.columnCap=1, kc = fx.columnBurstLit=0). The 32-frame runs start at tick 180 (k0t, kbt).
For comparison: runs/9/b0 (default) and runs/9/b2 (C103, fx.columnSoil=1). Code: runs/10/C108.patch (applied, not committed).

## Step 1: the arc detector

This is per-pixel sRGB HSV on the 1920x1080 frames. A pixel counts as arc when all of these hold:

- hue 18-30 deg, saturation 0.30-0.62, value 0.22-0.55, and r > b + 0.10.
- It is not within 220 px of a live flash (a hot blob of at least 400 px, with v > 0.85 and r > 0.80, warm).
- It is not within 30 px of a small hot light, such as a lamp or a fire.
- After a 1-px opening, it belongs to a blob of at least 60 px.

The hue band was cut from 18-40 to 18-30. At 40 it caught burst-lit khaki uniforms, hue about 33 (k0 f30, left trench).

I checked it on overlays of k0 f0, f10, f20, f30, f40, f50 and f60:

- It hits the arcs the critics named: the top-right trench helmets at f0, about (1560-1700, 440-520) (batch9 pair 4), and the bottom-centre burst at f50, about (800-1000, 780-1000) (pair 10).
- It hits the fountain loops at f60, about (740-960, 760-1000), and the top-right debris arcs late in the window (k0t f0, about (1470-1600, 100-250)).
- It does not hit the flashes, the lamps, the fires or the tracers.

Known false positive: a tan smoke cloud lit by a dimmer burst at f44-47, about (200-380, 280-520). It is present in every variant, b2 included (b2 f44: 13080 px, nearly all of it this cloud), so it does not affect the comparisons.

Arc pixels measured on k0 f50-63 (123k px): median sRGB (78, 56, 42). The ratios are g/r 0.718 and b/r 0.522.

## Step 2: the source

The arcs come from the Column book's own late drawing (Resources/VFX/Column.png, frames about 6-15: the column split into thin
arcs). The old column plays that drawing evenly over 1.8 s, so the arcs are what fills the last 1.2 s of every dry shell
(C103's note).

What colours them is the C57 night paint itself. It is not the burst's light, and it is not the moon:

- NightEarth, via PaintNight, sets _Tint = EarthTint(0.22) = EarthHue (1, 0.712, 0.508) times k. That hue is 25 deg with saturation 0.49.
- PaintNight also sets _Levels (2, 3), _Shade 0.5 and _Lit 0.6. The shader's colour is therefore _Tint x lerp(ink, 0.5, 0.6) = _Tint x (0.4 ink + 0.3).
- On the thin arcs the drawing's ink is light (about 0.9), so they are drawn about 30% brighter than the paint's mid value, at about (90, 64, 46).

The measured arc pixels have g/r 0.718 and b/r 0.522, which is EarthHue exactly. So nothing is added by the burst light (which would push them towards (1, 0.8, 0.5) orange-yellow) and nothing by the moon (which would push them towards blue). A 49%-saturated warm hue at value 0.33 sits over near-black smoke (29, 28, 27) and blue moonlit ground, and against those it reads as orange flame.

Why fx.columnBurstLit is inert:

- TWBurstLight is one global (NightLights.cs). It carries the brightest live burst only.
- It lives 0.48 s, falling as (1 - t)^2.
- The arcs are drawn 0.7-1.8 s into the column's life. By then _TWBurstColor is black, and the _BurstLit line multiplies zero.

Why b2 loses the arcs: fx.columnSoil does two things.

- SoilShape stops the drawing at frame 7.6 and collapses the card, so the split-arc frames never play for long.
- It paints at SoilValue 0.08 instead of 0.22 (about (33, 23, 17) on screen), which is below the arc band.

Both parts count. The value alone would make the arcs dark but still see-through. Other books are ruled out: the Burst and Smoke books are night-grey, Wings keeps its moonlit tan, and Splash is only for wet ground.

The critic's notes:

- The orange plumes are flat, single-tone, see-through arcs with no outline and no chunks in them. In every frame they read as "liquid spray or flame, never soil", and neither build fixes this; they only move the plumes.
- Where the plumes sit low in the smoke (kb over the top-right trench, f0 and f2), the men are marginally clearer.
- The left-burst plume in kb f56 is a wide flat smear that looks painted on (earth 4 -> 3).

Rule 6 check:

- Men went up (+0.33) and were never down by more than 1, nor down on average.
- Weight did not change (0, above the -0.5 floor).
- kb passes the letter of the rule, on the cap alone.

## Decision

The author's cause is wrong. fx.columnBurstLit is inert (the burst light is gone before the arcs are drawn), and the arcs are the C57 paint on the Column book's late split frames.

The only material gain is the cap over men. It moves the fountain off the helmets in the frames where the arcs cross a trench, and the men gain +1 there. It does not make the earth read as soil.

Default change pending: fx.columnCap=1, with fx.columnBurstLit left at 1 (inert).

For C109: the arcs themselves. Two options, measured by this detector, with b2 as the lower bound:

- A value below the arc band on the late frames only, for example fx.columnEarth falling with the column's age.
- Stopping the old column before its split frames, which is SoilShape's timing without C103's clods.
