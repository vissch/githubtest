# Concepts: a frog raises a flag when a trench is taken

The idea the owner accepted, 2026-10-08: every trench gets a pole; when it changes hands a frog hauls up the
taker's colour, red or blue, and the old flag flutters down. The front is told by flags, not by a list top left.

Four stagings to pick from, all drawn in SVG (nothing generated, nothing found): pages `a-stout-pole.svg`,
`b-tall-mast.svg`, `c-frog-is-the-pole.svg`, `d-flag-line.svg`, sheet `shown.jpg`, in the board's
`evidence/idea-a-frog-raises-a-flag-when-a-trench-is-ta/concept/`. Each page is the same bent parapet on mud, the
same standard-view framing (fov 25, pitch 25, zoom 30), the same rifleman at 2.0 m for scale, and the night
palette (dark blue moonlit mud, every light warm, blue only as moonlight); under each, three thumbs: the moment
before, during, after. The frog is the Bullfrog stand-in already in the Proving Ground (commit `8367c082`), so
what is being picked is the pole-and-flag staging — silhouette, size, timing — not the creature.

All four pages are drawn at **the same instant of the swap**: the new red is on its way up and the old blue is
already off its fastening. None shows the aftermath.

## The two colours: separated in value, not hue

Red `#ff8a5c` (relative luminance 0.402) against blue `#34639b` (0.151) — 2.66:1 apart, so the sides are told
apart by brightness, not by hue alone. Their darks, `#c04a2a` and `#1d3a63`, are 2.32:1 apart. Against the sky
band `#14202f` red is 7.1:1 and blue 2.7:1. `greyscale-check.jpg` beside the sheet is `shown.jpg` desaturated:
the two sides still read with the hue taken away, which is the test the first round failed (the old `#c8372c`
and `#3c6fa8` were 0.7% apart in luminance). Red is the light side because every light in the game is warm and
blue is moonlight.

## Sizes

Every page carries a SIZES panel and dimension lines on the drawing. The scale is the rifleman: **46 px = 1 m**.
No number is estimated; each is the drawn geometry divided by that.

The frog is one creature at one size, 1.0 m, in A, B and D. C's 2.0 m frog is the premise of C, not a drift.

## The four

| | What it is | What it costs to build | What it says |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A, a stout pole** (put first) | Short stout pole on the parapet; the frog at its base hauls a rope hand over hand; a wide stiff pennant 3.3 m across | Least: one short pole prop, one flag, one haul clip, one rope | "This trench is red now", as one broad block of colour |
| **B, a tall mast** | Tall thin mast; the frog is caught halfway up with the furled flag in its mouth, the mast head still bare; the old flag has just let go and is falling | More: a climb clip along the pole, and a loose falling flag (cloth, or a baked fall) | The tallest silhouette: it reads from furthest away, and the fall is the moment |
| **C, the frog is the pole** | No pole at all: the frog swells its throat and unrolls a banner out of its mouth like a tongue; the banner hangs as a wide sheet | Most: a swell and unroll on the frog itself, a hanging sheet, and no reuse of a pole prop | The most absurd and the most character; the frog, not the trench, is the thing watched |
| **D, a flag line** | Three short stakes along the trench, all one size; the frog runs the length swapping each; the old flag is off its stake and tumbling | Middle: one small stake prop used three times, a run along the trench, and a swap repeated | It says "the front", not "this trench": the shape of the line is the information |

**Why A is put first.** It reads at the standard view as one broad block of colour, which is the whole point of
the idea; both flags sit on one pole, so the swap is a single readable up-and-down; it is the least absurd
standing next to the riflemen; and it is the cheapest to build, one short pole, one flag and one haul clip. B is
the answer if the flags must read from the far zoom bands; D is the answer if the owner wants the front line
itself, not single trenches; D also carries the smallest flag of the four, 1.7 x 1.0 m.

Nothing is built until he picks: no flag mesh, no pole prop, no clip and no sim change. The pick goes to the brief

`docs/reference/decisions.md` is written when he has answered it.
